## Selected architecture
`Game.Structures.Runtime` owns canonical catalogue enumeration and production realization/presentation adapters. `PropShowcase` owns only browser UI, lifecycle, neutral support environment, and camera framing. Voxel-backed entries use production authoring/rendering; procedural/thin/world-object entries use production presenters. Presenter-owned geometry and support objects live on an independent world-space presentation root rather than the camera.

## Visual discriminator and selected fixes
Frame `SceneIssue/Screenshots/frame_002_t020.0.png` from run `33991512115` selected `Merchant Sign` (`decoration:19`, `ThinSurface`, presenter owned=1) while the right preview was visually blank. The production `DecorationThinSurfaceBatchBuilder` proves the mesh is correct: for `Facing +Z`, its normal and triangle winding face `+Z`. `DecorationShowcaseRealizer` gives wall-mounted entries `+Z`, while the old showcase framing placed the camera on `-Z`, so the camera viewed the semantic back face and backface culling hid the sign. The support surface sits behind the `+Z` thin surface, falsifying support-plane occlusion. The composition-only semantic-front framing fix made `Merchant Sign` visibly render from the correct side in exact run `33993868564` source `c98b1cbf49e2ec9da450d944b2545d05735c4fa5`.

Direct review of that fresh built-player artifact exposed a second acceptance-critical composition defect: floor-mounted realizations use `Facing +Y` as a mount normal, but the current camera treated it as a visual front and looked almost straight down at the prop. The `Game Table` and other floor representatives therefore lost useful silhouette/construction detail. Ceiling mounts analogously used `-Y` and were framed straight from below. This is showcase-owned camera composition, not production-content authoring. Commit `eeefd30565a32a0ad7fa61325d63efbe1ba29ddb` changes only framing semantics: vertical mount normals now preserve floor-versus-ceiling orientation while using elevated/underside three-quarter views; horizontal wall-facing entries remain close to their semantic front with a small tangent/elevation bias.
