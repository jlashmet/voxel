# Mountain Forest Town Briefs and Locked Pre-Concept Inventories

This file is the pre-concept lock. It was committed **before** any concept image generation for this SceneIssue. `Source-backed` means a named old-project map/story reference was recovered from `jlashmet/mounting-force@f37512e0175f46e14ad2d7aeb5ecea290af14a14` or is an explicit coordinator requirement. `Derived` means newly authored Project North Star art direction needed to make the location production-useful; it is not represented as historical fact.

## Kentridge
**Identity.** Welcoming mixed-trade town with a strong civic/market center and an undercurrent of food/supply hardship. The new version should feel colorful and inviting without erasing the original story's resource pressure.

**Architecture/materials.** Warm half-timber and cream plaster over fieldstone foundations; terracotta/brown tile; modest blue-painted civic trim; irregular but legible two-storey massing; larger church/warehouse silhouettes anchor the skyline.

**Palette.** Cream, warm oak, terracotta, muted civic blue, garden green, gray fieldstone; amber interior light.

**Culture/economy.** Inn, church, mayoral/civic functions, armor/magic commerce, warehouse/distribution, dense residences and gardens.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Walkable stone/dirt lanes, central well/market gathering area, carts/storage at warehouse, small retaining walls/fences, trees and kitchen gardens.

**Locked source-backed places.** Mayor House (`Art/kentridge-mayor-house.tmx`); Armor Shop (`kentridge-armor-shop.tmx`); Magic Shop (`kentridge-magic-shop.tmx`); Church (`kentridge-church.tmx`); Awon House (`kentridge-awon-house.tmx`); Rebecca House (`kentridge-rebecca-house.tmx`); Well (`kentridge-well.tmx`); Inn (`kentridge-inn.tmx`); Abandoned House (`kentridge-abandoned-house.tmx`); Sarah House (`kentridge-sarah-house.tmx`); Katie House (`kentridge-katie-house.tmx`); Warehouse/lower level (`kentridge-warehouse-lower.tmx`).

**Derived connective elements.** Central market stalls around the well; warehouse loading court; blue civic banners/sign paint; gardens and minor homes needed to connect source-backed places.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: town centered on well/market with church and warehouse legible; `exterior-01`: Magic Shop; `exterior-02`: Church; `interior-01`: Inn common room; `interior-02`: Magic Shop interior; `public-space`: well/market plaza with lanes, stalls and warehouse traffic.

## Hightown
**Identity.** Upland working town living under ration/distribution pressure. It should read tougher, steeper and more austere than Kentridge, but inhabited rather than ruined.

**Architecture/materials.** Rough gray-brown stone lower floors and retaining walls; dark weathered timber; steep slate roofs; narrow vertical fronts following grade; church/civic massing is severe and tall.

**Palette.** Slate blue-gray, dark brown, muted ochre, moss, off-white plaster, restrained iron red; warm but sparse interior lamplight.

**Culture/economy.** Working families, local shops, mayoral/distribution authority, church community. The source story directly groups Hightown with Kentridge as receiving inadequate food/supplies.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Terraced streets, stair lanes, retaining walls, drainage gutters, compact square/distribution point, defensible upland edges.

**Locked source-backed places.** Mayor House (dedicated Hightown mayor-house group in project manifest); Armor Shop (`hightown-armor-shop.tmx`); Magic Shop (`hightown-magic-shop.tmx`); Weapon Shop (`hightown-weapon-shop.tmx`); Timmy House (`hightown-timmy-house.tmx`); Church/under-church complex (source story/maps including `hightown-under-church2-*`); Hightown Cave (`hightown-cave.tmx`); Distribution scene/site (`hightown-distribution.txt`, Hightown distribution cut-scenes).

**Derived connective elements.** The distribution site is realized as a compact ration square/civic storehouse forecourt; terraced residences, stairs and retaining structures join the named places.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: steep terraced town with church and distribution square; `exterior-01`: Weapon Shop on stepped street; `exterior-02`: Church exterior; `interior-01`: Magic Shop interior adapted to narrow upland building; `interior-02`: Timmy House interior; `public-space`: ration/distribution square with stairs, retaining walls and civic queue infrastructure.

## Moordell
**Identity.** Prosperous, formal town of lords and nobles with privileged imperial alignment. Original dialogue explicitly calls it a town to the north, “full of Lords and nobles,” and contrasts its expected food/supply abundance with Kentridge/Hightown.

**Architecture/materials.** Dressed pale stone, fine plaster, carved dark timber, decorative metal, blue-gray slate; broad symmetrical facades and controlled rooflines; gardens/walls reinforce status.

**Palette.** Pale limestone, burgundy, restrained gold, deep green, slate blue-gray, dark polished timber.

**Culture/economy.** Nobility, formal civic administration, favored supply/distribution, wealth and magical/elite connections.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Wider ordered streets, formal civic square, stone walls/gates, landscaped edges, underground/wizard-trial layer associated with the Moordell overworld region.

**Locked source-backed places.** Moordell Building 1 (`Art/moordell-building1.tmx`); Mayor/civic interaction (`moordell-ask-mayor.txt`); Distribution scene/site (`Moordell-Distribution.txt`/`MoordellDistribution`); Gertrude House in Moordell underground (`overworld-moordell-underground-gertrude-house.tmx`); Moordell Underground (`overworld-moordell-underground.tmx`); Wizard Trials reference (`overworld-moordell-medrare-wizard-trials.txt`); grave/battle reference (`moordell-grave-battle-end.txt`).

**Derived connective elements.** Building 1 is treated as a representative noble townhouse; mayor/distribution functions are consolidated into a formal civic hall/forecourt for concept clarity; formal gate, square and garden vocabulary express source-backed wealth but are newly authored.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: ordered walled/noble town around civic axis; `exterior-01`: noble townhouse interpretation of Building 1; `exterior-02`: formal Mayor/Distribution Hall; `interior-01`: noble townhouse receiving room; `interior-02`: civic distribution hall interior; `public-space`: formal supply/civic square with gardens, dressed-stone paving and controlled gates.

## Rossdam
**Identity.** Dense trade town with a strong equipment/arms-commercial read. The recovered source backs three specialist shops; the heavier mercantile/martial character is a derived visual interpretation of that commercial concentration, not a claim about unrecovered lore.

**Architecture/materials.** Heavy stone-and-timber shop houses, brick/stone forge chimneys, forged iron brackets, deep eaves, red-brown tile, brass accents, large ground-floor shop openings.

**Palette.** Iron gray, charcoal, brick red, tan leather, dark oak, brass, parchment cream.

**Culture/economy.** Armor, weapons and magic commerce; merchant traffic; workshops and loading/storage courts.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Broad merchant street, cart/loading bays, shop signs, covered arcades, robust gate/bridge approach as **derived** connective infrastructure.

**Locked source-backed places.** Armor Shop (`rossdam-armor-shop.tmx`); Magic Shop (`rossdam-magic-shop.tmx`); Weapon Shop (`rossdam-weapon-shop.tmx`); Henry quest reference (`rossdam-henry-give-quest.txt`); Rossdam overworld approach/region (`overworld-to-rossdam.tmx`, `overworld-rossdam.tmx`).

**Derived connective elements.** Trade square, forge/service courts, outer gate and merchant warehouses. These express the shop-heavy source inventory but are not old-game named places.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: compact trade town focused on merchant spine; `exterior-01`: Armor Shop; `exterior-02`: Weapon Shop; `interior-01`: Armor Shop/workshop floor; `interior-02`: Magic Shop interior; `public-space`: merchant street/trade square with carts, signs, arcades and ironwork.

## Cambridge
**Identity.** **Coordinator-canonical water town.** It must read immediately as water-dependent even with labels removed: docks, waterfront architecture, bridges/water crossings and water-oriented public infrastructure are mandatory.

**Provenance note.** No Cambridge-named map/interior was recovered in the pinned old-project archive. Therefore the water-town requirement is source-backed by the coordinator; the exact place names below are deliberately marked **derived Project North Star canon** rather than old-game recovery.

**Architecture/materials.** Pale waterfront stone lower levels, weathered timber upper levels/decks, blue slate, copper/verdigris roofs and gutters, painted shutters, pile-supported additions, arcades opening to quays.

**Palette.** Sea green, weathered blue, pale stone, warm dock timber, copper/verdigris, cream sails/canvas, amber lanterns.

**Culture/economy.** Ferries, fishing, boat repair, waterborne market trade and civic water management — derived from the required water-town identity.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Quays, docks, stepped water stairs, multiple bridges, narrow canals/inlets, drainage/waterworks, mooring posts, small boat traffic.

**Locked required/derived places.** **Required:** docks/waterfront; bridges/water crossings; water-oriented civic infrastructure. **Derived canonical places for this pack:** Harbor Hall & Waterworks; Bridge Market; Ferryman/Boatwright House; Tideglass Inn; North Quay Warehouse; public water stairs.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: town spanning water with several bridges and dock districts; `exterior-01`: Harbor Hall & Waterworks; `exterior-02`: Tideglass Inn/Boatwright waterfront facade; `interior-01`: Tideglass Inn interior overlooking quay; `interior-02`: Harbor Hall waterworks/permit chamber; `public-space`: Bridge Market + public water stairs/docks.

## Wharfington
**Identity.** Forest-edge wharf/exchange town where water/road transport meets timber and forest goods. Its river/wharf/forest-exchange treatment is **derived** from the recovered/reference name and Mountain Forest role; the pinned project did not yield a stable set of Wharfington-named interiors, so this file avoids pretending newly coined building names are historical.

**Architecture/materials.** Robust timber frames, fieldstone piers/foundations, shingle roofs, rope/canvas canopies, open-sided sheds and long low warehouses; more rustic and forest-integrated than Cambridge.

**Palette.** Forest green, river blue-gray, honey/amber wood, gray fieldstone, rope tan, muted rust, warm resin-gold light.

**Culture/economy.** Timber, forest produce, river/road transfer, repair/craft yards — derived production identity for the Mountain Forest exchange role.

**Environment/infrastructure.** Working wharf/landing, timber yards, forest road junction, modest bridge, muddy/stone service lanes, sheds and warehouses embedded directly against dense trees.

**Locked required/derived places.** No old-project named interior is asserted. **Derived canonical places for this pack:** Forest Wharf; Timber Exchange Hall; Wayfarer Inn; Ropewright/Repair Shed; River Warehouse; Junction Commons/bridge.

**Six locked concepts.** `overall`: wharf settlement merging into dense forest and road junction; `exterior-01`: Timber Exchange Hall; `exterior-02`: Wayfarer Inn + River Warehouse frontage; `interior-01`: Wayfarer Inn interior; `interior-02`: Timber Exchange Hall interior; `public-space`: Forest Wharf/Junction Commons with bridge, log/timber handling and road transition.

## Cross-town acceptance lock
No concept may substitute a generic building for its listed subject. Every concept should show at least one town-specific infrastructure cue in addition to its primary subject. The six sets must stay coherent under the shared Project North Star storybook language while remaining immediately distinguishable by silhouette, material, palette, economy and terrain.
